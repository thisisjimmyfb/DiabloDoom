# MUSE.md — Integrate a Muse avatar into DiabloDoom's status-bar face

> Audience: a Muse integrating a **user's** avatar into DiabloDoom.
> This is NOT for the farm game-playing agent — that agent follows
> `agent/SYSTEM_PROMPT.md` and `agent/PROMPT.md` and ignores this file.
> If you are here because a person asked you to put their avatar in
> Doom, this is your guide. If you are here to play the game, stop
> and read the farm prompts instead.

## The deal, in one paragraph

No engine change is needed for a working integration. The status-bar
face is 42 WAD lumps loaded by name (`src/doom/st_stuff.c`,
`ST_loadUnloadGraphics`); a PWAD supplied with `-file` overrides any
of them, and everything you don't override falls back to the IWAD.
So you generate the face patches, pack them into a PWAD, and launch
with `-file`. Remove `-file` and the vanilla Doomguy face returns —
that is the fallback, and it always works. The engine never decodes
a `.webp` and never scans for avatars: that design is dead and must
not be revived. You generate the assets; the engine just loads them.

## Default PWAD and first-time setup

This repo ships a default avatar so a first-time user starts with a
working face instead of vanilla Doomguy: **Jolly**, the default Muse
avatar, as `agent/jolly.wad` (42 lumps, 26 distinct images — straights
pain-graded and distinct, transient faces share across bands by
design since marks were removed; sized to fill the entire face
window, frame included; built from the Jolly hatch reference with
a further-back framing, and smoke-tested in-game). First-time setup is one copy:

```sh
mkdir -p avatar && cp agent/jolly.wad avatar/
./build/src/chocolate-doom -iwad <path-to-iwad> -file avatar/jolly.wad
```

`avatar/` (repo root) is the live slot: the file the game actually
loads. It is gitignored on purpose — replacing
`avatar/jolly.wad` with your own PWAD (generated by the
procedure below) is how you customize, and a personal avatar must
never be committed. Only copy on first setup: re-copying after you
customize would overwrite your face with Jolly again. The committed
default stays in `agent/`; the live copy stays in `avatar/`.

## The 42 slots

The face widget (`STlib_initMultIcon`, position 143,168) shows
`faces[st_faceindex]`, picked every tic by `ST_updateFaceWidget`.
There are 8 expressions, and the first 6 are graded across 5 pain
levels (health bands from `ST_calcPainOffset`):

| Pain band | Health |
|---|---|
| 0 | 100–80 |
| 1 | 79–60 |
| 2 | 59–40 |
| 3 | 39–20 |
| 4 | 19–0 (dead takes over at 0) |

Per band `i` (0–4), the lump names are:

| Expression | When shown | Lumps |
|---|---|---|
| Straight (idle) | Nothing else active; 3 variants cycled randomly every ~0.5 s | `STFSTi0`, `STFSTi1`, `STFSTi2` |
| Turn right | Attacker to the player's right | `STFTRi0` |
| Turn left | Attacker to the player's left | `STFTLi0` |
| Ouch | One hit drops health by more than 20 | `STFOUCHi` |
| Evil grin | Just picked up a new weapon (2 seconds) | `STFEVLi` |
| Rampage | Head-on attacker, self-damage, or sustained firing | `STFKILLi` |

Plus two singles with no pain variants:

| Expression | When shown | Lump |
|---|---|---|
| God | God mode cheat or invulnerability powerup | `STFGOD0` |
| Dead | Health 0 (highest priority) | `STFDEAD0` |

Total: 5 bands × 8 slots + 2 = 42 lumps. Priority order is:
dead > evil grin > turned head > straight ahead, with god and
rampage slotted in by their own rules — you don't need to implement
any of this, you only need to fill the slots.

### Distinct expressions (required for straights; sharing allowed for transient faces)

Every straight face gets its OWN image. Do NOT duplicate bytes
across straight slots — no sharing across pain bands, and no
sharing across the 3 straight variants in a band. This is a
requirement, not an enhancement, and it comes from a shipped
mistake: v1 duplicated one image per expression across all bands
and straight variants, and the result was that 50% health looked
exactly like 100% and the idle face never animated. The engine's
only periodic face change is the straight-variant cycle (~0.5 s)
plus the band progression as health falls; duplicating straights
flattens both, so a duplicated straight set reads as a frozen
face. Transient faces (turn, ouch, grin, rampage) MAY share
across bands when they have no band-specific feature left to
differ by — that sharing is by design, not a bug, since the
damage marks that used to differentiate them were removed
(see below). Concretely:

- Each band's straight face must visibly differ from the other
  bands (pain grading: grimacing mouth, narrowing eyes as the
  band rises).
- The 3 straight variants within each band must visibly differ
  (subtle eye shifts are enough — that is what makes idle cycle).
- Turn, ouch, grin, and rampage must differ from each other
  (different expressions, not the same image across expressions),
  but need not differ per band: the shipped default shares turn
  bands 0–2 and shares ouch, grin, and rampage across all bands
  (42 lumps, 26 distinct images), because marks were the only
  band differentiator for those faces and marks were removed.
  Turn bands 3–4 stay distinct through eye narrowing.
- God and dead are singles; they differ from each other and from
  every band face by construction.

Damage marks were tried (v4) and removed (v4 fix, 2026-10-28,
per the user: "they don't look good on pixel art"). At 35×31 a
1 px mark reads as a brown dot, and in the further-back framing
most of the v2 head-fill mark coordinates fell OFF the face
(above/beside the head, on hood/body/background). Do not re-add
marks without a new design the user approves.

Verify distinctness mechanically (hash all 42 patch byte strings;
expect the known sharing groups above, and treat any other
collision as a bug), and visually (a labeled montage of all 42
before shipping).

## Input: the avatar reference image

You need one front-facing head image of the user's avatar: eyes and
mouth visible, head roughly centered, high enough resolution to
downscale cleanly (work at ~10× the final size, e.g. 350×310, then
downscale to 35×31). The user supplies it, or you use your own
current avatar image if the user is asking about your face.

Know this before you start: avatar assets carry no facial-expression
variants. Their animated variants are activity states (idle,
working, making something, milestone), not faces. You are
synthesizing expressions from one static reference — that is the
whole job, and it is a drawing job, not a decoding job.

## Generation procedure

Work at the final patch size: **35 wide × 31 tall**, offsets
**(0,0)**. The STBAR face window, bevel frame included, is 35×31
(exactly the STFB faceback panel size), so a narrower patch leaves
the bright bevel frame showing around it as gaps — a light avatar
against the frame makes those gaps glaring, and the face reads as
not filling its box. Size the patch to the whole window (the
widget draws any size patch; vanilla faces themselves vary from
24 to 31 wide), and crop the head tight so it fills the frame
rather than floating in it.

1. **Crop to the head** and scale to your working size (~300×290).
   Keep the head centered and front-facing; every expression below
   is derived from this one crop. Framing is the user's call: a
   tight crop makes the head fill the frame with minimal body and
   minimal margin, while a looser head-and-shoulders crop (head
   ~70% of frame height, shoulders below, light margin above)
   reads as the avatar a little further back. Whichever framing
   is picked, derive ALL expressions from that one base at final
   size — do not mix bases with different framing. Mixing was
   tried and rejected: one base's eyes sat at y~10 with mouth at
   y~13 while the other's sat at y15-16 with mouth at y18-19,
   so the face jumped 5-6 px on every expression change.
2. **Derive the 8 expressions.** Use judgment; the requirement is
   that all 8 read as different at 35×31, not any particular pixel
   layout:
   - Straight: the reference as-is.
   - Turn left / turn right: shift the pupils (and, if needed, the
     whole eye/mouth block) toward the side, further than the
     straight idle shift (v4: turns shift 2 px, straights shift
     1 px), so a turn reads as a turn, not just idle variation.
   - Ouch: open mouth, widened eyes — a wince.
   - Evil grin: asymmetric smirk.
   - Rampage: gritted teeth, narrowed eyes, angled brows.
   - God: a radiant / invulnerable treatment of the straight face.
   - Dead: closed or X eyes, slack mouth.
3. **Grade the 5 bands and vary the straights.** From each base
   expression, build its 5 band images: band 0 stays clean, and
   bands 1–4 get progressively grimier (grimacing mouth on the
   straight face, narrowing eyes at low health). Build 3 straight
   variants per band (eye shifts: center, 1 px left, 1 px right).
   Straights must all differ from each other; transient band
   images may share when they have no band feature left to differ
   by — see "Distinct expressions" above.
4. **Draw thin features at final resolution.** Mouths in particular
   must be drawn at 35×31, not downscaled: thin mouths vanish in a
   NEAREST downscale and the face goes blank, and a thin smile
   can fragment into separate dots. Downscale the head
   with NEAREST, inpaint any fragmented source mouth, then
   draw/retouch the mouth (and any thin eye
   detail) at final size, centred on the source mouth's position.
   If the user asks to move the mouth, move the drawn mouth —
   do not re-crop the source, or the framing changes with it.
5. **Make god and dead exactly mirror-symmetric.** Asymmetric god
   and dead faces read as broken at this size; symmetry is a
   requirement for those two, not a preference.
6. **Quantize to PLAYPAL.** Read the PLAYPAL lump from the IWAD
   (`doom.wad` or `freedoom1.wad`) and map every RGB pixel to its
   nearest PLAYPAL entry. Check the quantized result visually before
   shipping it: pale colors map badly (light creams collapse to
   gray-pink), and a face that reads in RGB can go muddy in palette.
   If it reads wrong, adjust the source colors — do not ship a face
   you have only judged in RGB.
7. **Encode as Doom patches.** Each patch is a `patch_t`: width 35,
   height 31, offsets (0,0), column offsets, and per-column
   posts. Transparency is encoded the Doom way: background pixels
   simply have no posts in their columns, so the face composites
   over the status bar behind it. (An opaque patch that fills its
   box needs no transparency posts.) Precedent in this repo:
   `assets/make_pistol_sprite.py` builds a genuine `patch_t` from
   an image with PLAYPAL from the IWAD — read it before writing
   your encoder and follow the same approach.
8. **Pack the PWAD.** Write all 42 lumps with the exact names from
   the table above, following the distinctness rule above
   (straights distinct, transient sharing allowed by design). Name the file
   something like `<avatar>_face.wad`. Keep a user's personal avatar
   PWAD with the user — do not commit it to this repo. The one
   exception is the shipped Jolly default (`agent/jolly.wad`); a
   personal avatar lives in the gitignored `avatar/` slot, never in git.

## Launch and verify

From the repo root, after `scripts/build.sh`:

```sh
./build/src/chocolate-doom -iwad <path-to-iwad> -file <avatar>_face.wad
```

With the shipped default (after the first-time copy above), that is
`-file avatar/jolly.wad`.

Check each trigger in-game, watching the status-bar face:

- Idle: the avatar's straight face, cycling its variants.
- Damage from the side: the face turns toward the attacker.
- One big hit (20+ damage): ouch.
- Pick up a new weapon: evil grin for ~2 seconds.
- Hold fire: rampage.
- God mode / invulnerability: god face.
- Die: dead face.
- Relaunch without `-file`: vanilla Doomguy returns (fallback check).

If a face never appears, the usual cause is a lump-name typo — the
loader looks up names exactly, and a misspelled lump silently falls
back to the IWAD face for that slot. Check names first.

## What is NOT built yet (do not promise it)

- **Hi-res RGB overlay.** The face draws through the paletted patch
  path (`V_DrawPatch` via the `w_faces` multicon widget). No RGB
  overlay draw path exists in this codebase, so a pale avatar will
  read imperfectly — that is the known PLAYPAL limitation from
  step 6, not something this guide can work around. An overlay is
  future engine work.
- **Loader slot-sharing.** The engine loads 42 names; the shipped
  default fills all 42 slots with 26 distinct images (transient
  sharing by design, see "Distinct expressions" above), so
  further sharing is unnecessary there. Loading 8 images and sharing them across slots
  needs a loader change — future engine work for anyone who wants
  fewer images, and it must not be used to reintroduce duplication
  (see "Distinct expressions" above).
- **File-based loading.** There is no "drop PNGs in a folder" path;
  the shipped default is a PWAD via `-file`.

## Worked example (how this procedure was validated)

The default Muse avatar (Jolly) was used to validate this procedure:
a 30×29 six-state preview set (normal / look left / look right /
pain / god / dead) was generated first, and the lessons above —
god and dead symmetric, mouths at final resolution, pale creams
failing in PLAYPAL — all came from that pass. That validation is
now shipped as `agent/jolly.wad` v3 (all 42 lumps distinct, pain-graded, and
sized 35×31 to fill the entire face window, frame included). v2
sized 30×29 to the window's dark interior and left the bevel frame
showing as gaps; v1 shipped the duplication default
and was pulled after it was found to make 50% health look exactly
like 100% with a frozen idle face — that failure is why distinct
expressions are required, not optional. v3 was verified two ways:
a labeled montage of all 42 faces (grading progression and idle
variants judged before shipping) and an in-game smoke test
(launched with `-file`, the status-bar face shows Jolly at 100%
health filling the entire window with no frame showing, and relaunching without `-file` returns
vanilla Doomguy). v4 (2026-10-28) replaces v3's head-fill framing with
the further-back framing above: the Normal base comes directly
from the original hatch image with a loose crop, all 42 faces
are derived from that one base at final size (sheet mixing
rejected for the eye/mouth jump above), the Normal straight
mouth is a 5 px smile drawn at final size, 1 px lower than the
source mouth, per the user's pick, God is the symmetrised Normal
with a wider 7 px smile, and Dead is X eyes with a slack mouth.
v4 was verified the same two ways: a labeled montage of all 42
and an in-game smoke test. v4 fix (2026-10-28) removes the damage
marks entirely (1 px marks read as brown dots and fell off-face
in the further-back framing, per the user's pick), gentles the
evil-grin smirk (flat 3 px, up 1 px, after the 2 px rise read too
crooked), and widens the band-4 straight grimace to 7 px with a
3 px centre gap (total width unchanged). With marks gone, the 42
lumps collapse to 26 distinct images by design (turn bands 0–2
share, ouch/grin/rampage share across all bands); straights stay
distinct through mouths and eye narrowing. Verified the same two
ways: labeled montage and in-game smoke test.
